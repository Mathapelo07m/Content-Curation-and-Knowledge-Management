01

The tool + the thinking

Content curation is not hoarding bookmarks. Like any good resource, a link only becomes curated knowledge once you have evaluated it, organised it and shared it for a purpose.

Collecting

Finding and saving information — a link, an article, a video. The raw material.

Curating

Evaluating, organising and sharing the best of it around a theme and a teaching goal — that is knowledge management.

Fifty saved tabs is just collecting. A themed, evaluated Wakelet collection my learners can actually use is curation. The question is never “what can I save?” — it is “what is worth sharing, and why?”

03

The three tools I used — and how

I curated with three tools that work together: Wakelet to collect and evaluate resources, Padlet to organise and showcase them, and GitHub to publish interactive learning games built from the same curated content. Here is each one and exactly how I used it — tap through to the live result.

🗂️

Wakelet

Collect & evaluate

Wakelet is a free platform for building visual collections of links, text and media. I used it as my main curation hub, building three themed collections of CAPS-aligned ICT and Life Orientation resources. For every link I added a short note on why it earns its place and how I would use it in class — so each collection shows my professional judgement, not just a list.

  • Three themed, evaluated collections
  • Verified, credible South African sources
  • A justification note on every resource

Padlet

Organise & share

Padlet is a collaborative board for arranging and sharing resources visually. I used it to pull my curated material together into one board — "The Future-Ready South African Classroom" — laid out in topic columns so peers and learners can navigate the whole collection at a glance and respond with comments and reactions.

  • Organised into topic sections (shelf layout)

  • Comments & reactions on for interaction

Listing vs. evaluating

This is the heart of curation. A resource that is only listed is not curated — it needs your professional judgement attached.

Listing

“Here is a link to the SADAG website.”

Evaluating

“SADAG (sadag.org) is South Africa’s largest mental-health NGO. I curated it for my Life Orientation wellbeing collection because it is a credible, current, local source with free learner support lines — I’d use it to open a lesson on help-seeking.”
